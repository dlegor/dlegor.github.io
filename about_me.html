About me

Hey, this is Daniel Legorreta, and this is my personal site. I live and have built my career over the years in the world of data. I find it difficult to apply any of the standard industry labels to myself—such as data scientist, ML engineer, MLOps specialist, data engineer, or any new title that might emerge in the coming months. This is because I have worked in various roles throughout my career. While I have focused more on model deployment and inference in recent years, I continue to explore how technology has evolved—from the fundamentals of data handling to the recent rise of LLMs, which have taken center stage in areas like code generation.

While technology is constantly changing, I personally believe that the fundamentals remain the foundation for any project. I try to apply my expertise across various areas in every project—combining the perspectives of a data engineer, an ML specialist, and a data scientist—to add value to the initiatives I’m involved in. Ultimately, having a "bias for action" helps you be more productive.

I typically tackle the following core challenges:

  • Implementation of robust machine learning and deep learning models.
  • Architecting models and infrastructure for real-time anomaly detection.
  • Engineering high-performance data processing pipelines for distributed systems.
  • Driving product innovation using cutting-edge NLP techniques and LLMs (chatbots, semantic search, intent classification).

A lifelong learner, I am enthusiastic about researching and implementing cutting-edge techniques and algorithms in personal projects. I prioritize staying up-to-date with the latest technological advancements and have a deep appreciation for mathematics. My mathematical interests include algebraic topology, geometry, probability, and large graph theory, fields that I recognize as increasingly integral to Machine Learning, Deep Learning, and Computer Science.

Below are some research links on the relationship between mathematics and computer science:

  • Algebraic topology and data.
  • Geometry and deep learning.
  • Everything in machine learning is probability, so I always recommend reading Kevin Murphy's book.
  • Graphs and large-scale graph learning — a huge topic, so it is impossible to give just one reference.

Background and Experience

My major was in pure mathematics; I graduated from the IPN in Mexico. I obtained solid foundations in mathematics and physics, which helped me to develop professionally in applications of mathematics to various problems in industry.

My thesis work was on the application of algebraic topology in economics (the paradox of social choice) under the supervision of Ph.D. Jesus Gonzales Espino.

In parallel, I worked as a research assistant in Time Series and Complex Systems applications at the UPIITA-IPN Complex Systems Laboratory, under the supervision of Ph.D. Lev Guzmán. I also worked as a research assistant in Mathematical Analysis under the supervision of Ph.D. Ramírez de Arellano.

I learned to program in C, C++, Python, R, SQL and Bash before finishing college, and since 2008 I have implemented and developed components and models for various business cases. Since 2012 I have specialized in forecasting models, developing them for various companies, including Softtek, where they predicted the demand for more than 100 applications and systems.

After 2015 I specialized in Machine Learning and started using Deep Learning for different problems. In general, most of my projects have been on three topics:

  • Anomaly detection (fraud, risk, anomalies).
  • Segmentation (customer classification, post or text categorization, etc.).
  • Forecasting models (time series, multiple time series, regression models, etc.).

Recently I have worked on projects that require the implementation of NLP techniques, computer vision, deep learning and LLMs (agents).
